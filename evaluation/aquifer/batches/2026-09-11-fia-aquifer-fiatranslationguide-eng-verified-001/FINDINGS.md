# What this FIA guide requires of the experience

This source-local reading examines the English guide for **1 Chronicles 1:1–7**, not the whole FIA collection. Its six stages form a facilitated group learning process: listening, contextual discussion, scene construction, drama, vocabulary decisions and retelling. A useful experience must preserve the work people do between recordings, including their choices and uncertainty.

The complete rendered guide was read. Linked resources, audio playback, licensing, field usability and theological or translation correctness have not been validated. These findings are analytical support for the proposed app direction, not new product decisions.

## The workflow is more than a sequence of media files

The opening uses repeated listening, preferably across three translations when available, followed by discussion. Later stages return to the easiest-understood recording. Participants pause to consider meaning, compare cultural practices, identify scenes, make language choices, act out perspectives and retell. The pauses have a purpose; the rendered source does not supply their duration or machine-readable timing.

This supports the proposed pause/discuss/resume behavior in FIA-004. It also limits a simplistic interpretation of FIA-007: a deterministic sequence can organize stages and resources, but should not force a group to advance before its discussion or decision is finished. No runtime AI requirement follows from this source.

## Local choices and their consequences need to remain visible

The guide asks the group to decide whether to introduce the genealogy with a heading, how to express relationships, how to distinguish individuals from people groups, and whether names should retain sound, convey meaning or include geographic explanation. Several alternatives depend on local language and existing translation work. A choice in the current session may require consistent changes elsewhere.

This qualifies the app's proposed consume-only boundary in FIA-001. The resource itself prompts translation-related decisions and creates activities such as drawing or storyboarding. A resource app can support those activities without implementing translation recording or checking. Excluding a feature from the app must not remove an instruction from the FIA method or pretend the user's wider workflow stops at the app's edge.

## Cultural comparison is part of the task

The guide connects ancient genealogies with how participants remember ancestry, trace family relationships and identify peoples or places. It distinguishes paternal, maternal and combined descent conventions rather than treating one local pattern as universal. It then asks how the passage can be expressed naturally for the group's audience.

That is evidence of an intended cultural-learning activity, not proof that the activity is effective in every oral culture. Target-language and field review remain necessary. This one English guide cannot establish localization completeness or a validated oral-user interface.

## Examples should not become compulsory answers

The drama stage assigns storyteller and audience roles. An uninterrupted pass establishes the whole passage, followed by a pass that pauses for thoughts and feelings. Suggested responses illustrate a range of perspectives; they are not a single answer key.

An app presentation should preserve the difference between prompts and examples. Showing suggested responses before participants reflect could undermine the activity. That is a proposed design implication, not an observed usability result.

## The final retelling has three distinct levels of participation

Participants first build the passage together in short portions, with correction allowed. Each then tells the complete passage without interruption, using memory aids if helpful. Finally the group can combine strong portions from different tellings into a shared version and practice it individually.

Reducing this to one chosen speaker or one final recording would lose both individual participation and the option to combine contributions. The source does not require recording or uploading that final version.

## Relationship to the proposed claims

This guide provides concrete support for internalization and articulation (FIA-001), communal participation (FIA-002), access to related resources (FIA-003), and intentional pause/resume flow (FIA-004). It provides a reason to preserve reference structure and investigate timing metadata (FIA-009). It supports examining a predictable staged flow under FIA-007 while preserving optional steps and human choice.

It provides no device test for phone/portrait behavior, offline storage, conversational control or compression (FIA-005, FIA-006, FIA-008 and FIA-010). Those remain proposed comparison hypotheses. Nor does the rendered body prove that timing metadata is absent from the original upstream format.
